P-Flow's MRI advantage is the strongest repeated pattern. Its median image
error is approximately 0.85% in both completed MRI groups, compared with
10.40% and 66.88% for DLR. At the 5% threshold, P-Flow succeeds while DLR
fails on 11 versus 0 reverse cases for one circle, and 15 versus 1 for two
circles. Exact paired discordance tests remain below 0.05 after Holm adjustment
over the 15 pairwise method comparisons at this threshold in the five complete
groups (adjusted p approximately 0.0127 and 0.0073). These are exploratory
analyses of one model and one paired initialization per image, not an estimate
of robustness across retraining or independent source seeds.

DLR is close to exact D-Flow on one-circle CT. Its small advantage at 128px
does not establish superiority. With two circles, success counts fall for
all three methods; P-Flow's MRI performance is more resilient. Resolution
and circle-count comparisons change the trained model and dataset, so they
cannot isolate rank adequacy or spatial-discretization effects.

The strongest DLR concern is **128px three-circle CT**: among 26 completed
triples, D/P/DLR success counts are **4/10/0**, with DLR median image error
79.57% and 24/26 severe errors above 20%. This group is incomplete and may be
completion-biased. The previous sweep also had **0/26 DLR successes on these
same cases**, so the failure pattern predates previous-V reuse. It does not
by itself prove that rank 8 is insufficient. The unfinished 250px one-circle
MRI group has 11/21, 21/21, and 13/21 successes, respectively. No 64px cases
have finished in this restarted sweep at this snapshot.

## What the stopping histories show

Among the 150 cases in complete acquisitions, DLR has 72 severe errors (>20%).
Of these, **70 stalled**, with median terminal attempt 21; only two exhausted
the 100-attempt budget. Both of those two still reduced the objective by more
than 1% over their final 20 attempts. Increasing outer iterations alone will
not restart the other 70 cases.

**66/70 severe DLR stalls used all 30 trials on their final failed search**;
the other four used 29. Thus the geometric search budget is now frequently
active, unlike the earlier interpolation audit, where failures occurred
before reaching 30 trials. This makes the backtracking budget a concrete
diagnostic target. Exhausting it does not establish that additional trials
would yield a representable, objective-decreasing step.

63/72 severe DLR cases are also severe for exact D-Flow. None previously
achieved <=5% image error, and their final measurement residuals are all
above 8.29%. The dominant observed failure is failing to fit the measurements;
these histories do not show late overfitting after an accurate reconstruction.
They also do not establish low-rank approximation as the sole cause.

## What changed after the restart

The old/new comparison uses exactly the same 150 cases in the five complete
groups, not aggregate counts from differently completed subsets.

| Method | Prior successes | Current successes | Rescued / regressed |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| D-Flow | 68/150 | 74/150 | 10 / 4 |
| P-Flow | 82/150 | 90/150 | 11 / 3 |
| DLR D-Flow | 67/150 | 70/150 | 12 / 9 |

DLR's mean image error falls from 46.68% to 44.13%, and its median from
26.35% to 18.32%. Its success gain is only 2 percentage points, with an
exploratory paired bootstrap 95% interval of [-4, 8] points and paired
discordance p=0.664. This does not yet establish a general accuracy improvement.
Geometric halving and V reuse changed together for DLR, so their individual
contributions cannot be identified from this comparison.

All 197 available old/new pairs have matching checkpoint, source, acquisition
and sketch-seed metadata, and bitwise-identical truths, original sources,
clean measurements, observations and masks. Complete-group medians reproduce
the original worker summaries. Objective histories are nonincreasing. The
analysis ran on CPU without altering or interrupting the GPU 1 sweep.
