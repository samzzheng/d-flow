What the last two columns mean. Every TV setting is compared with no TV on the same 20 cases: five samples at each of four resolutions. “Samples with lower image MSE” counts cases where TV improved reconstruction accuracy. “Samples with lower solution TV” counts cases where TV produced a smoother reconstruction. These are independent tests: one sample can count in both columns, one column, or neither, so the totals should not be added together. Lower solution TV is evidence of reduced oscillation, but it does not automatically imply lower image error. The counts are binary and do not show improvement magnitude; the mean and median MSE columns provide that information.
Result. Across all four resolutions, TV weight 3×10−6 gave the lowest overall mean image MSE, 6.450e-05, compared with 1.008e-03 without TV (93.6% lower), and won 15/20 paired comparisons. TV weight 10−4 gave a similar overall mean MSE of 6.694e-05, reduced mean solution TV by 70.7%, and reduced solution TV in 19/20 pairs.

Experiment: four resolutions, five samples per resolution, 750 outer LBFGS steps per sample, LR 1.0, TV weight 10−4, no line search, and five Euler steps. Timing used one GPU process, with the first 10 iterations of each sample excluded as warm-up.
